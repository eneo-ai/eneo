# Oversee Spaces as an Administrator

import { Callout } from "nextra/components";

Organisation administrators can see and manage every shared space in the organisation under **Admin → Spaces**, without being members. You see each space's settings, members, resources and usage, you can correct its membership and you can review its web widgets. The space's documents, questions, answers and conversations stay with its members: to see them you join the space with a role and a written reason, which the space's members can see and the audit log always records.

**Spaces** is the first item under **Governance** in the admin menu. It is available to organisation administrators, that is, users whose role has the **Admin** permission.

<Callout type="info">
  Oversight covers shared spaces only. Personal spaces are never listed. The
  organisation space is not listed either, because organisation administrators
  are already members of it.
</Callout>

## What administrators can see

### The list of spaces

**Admin → Spaces** lists every shared space in one table. For each space it shows:

- the name and description, with a badge when the space has no administrator or a web widget is waiting for activation;
- the security classification, when security classifications are enabled;
- its administrators;
- the number of members (people, directly or through groups) and groups;
- its resources: assistants, apps, knowledge sources and active web widgets;
- when it was last active, as a period: past week, past month, past 3 months, more than 3 months ago, or no activity;
- your own membership: not a member, your role, your role through a group, or that you joined through oversight. When you are a member, **Open space** takes you to the space itself.

Search by name, description or administrator, filter on your membership (**Where I'm a member**, **Where I'm not a member**) and on what needs attention (**Needs attention**, **No administrator**, **Widget waiting for activation**), and sort by name, members or last activity. The filters are kept in the page address, so the browser's back button and a bookmark return to the same view. Spaces you are a member of also appear in your space selector as usual; the others appear only here.

### A single space

Choose a space's name to open it. The page starts with your own relation to the space (see [Join a space](#join-a-space)), followed by a summary: classification, administrators, members, last activity, questions and active users over the past 30 days, questions through the space's web widgets, stored knowledge and when the space was created. Five tabs follow:

- **Settings**: how long conversations are kept, the space's language, embedding and transcription models, whether web search and image generation are on, and its tools (MCP servers).
- **Assistants and apps**: for each assistant, whether it is published, its model, the knowledge sources it uses, the number of attached files, its tools, whether insights and full logging are on, how long it keeps conversations, its web widget and its instructions. Apps show their models, instructions and retention, and group chats show their status, number of assistants and whether insights are on. Instructions stay collapsed until you choose **Show instructions**.
- **Knowledge**: the knowledge sources the space owns, with type, number of documents or pages, size, last update and the assistants that use them. Websites also show how often they are fetched, whether they require sign-in and whether fetching has stopped after repeated errors. The tab also counts the sources the space uses from the organisation space.
- **Members**: people and groups with their roles; see [Manage members](#manage-members).
- **Web widgets**: the space's widgets, their assistant and status, and when activation was requested.

Apart from membership and widget decisions, oversight is read-only: you cannot change a space's settings, assistants or knowledge, or delete a space, from these pages.

### What is never shown

The oversight pages never show:

- document titles or contents, or the pages fetched from a website;
- questions, answers, conversation names, feedback, or the input and output of app runs;
- the names of attached files, only how many there are;
- website sign-in details or tool server secrets;
- the folders and paths behind an integration; a OneDrive source is shown as **OneDrive (personal folder)** without its name;
- who created an assistant, app or knowledge source;
- the exact time of the last activity.

Assistant and app instructions are shown, because they decide how an assistant behaves and what a public web widget answers. Instructions can contain personal data; see [Privacy](#privacy).

## Content requires membership

A space's content is for its members: documents and their titles, questions, answers, conversations and attached files. Oversight does not change that. The oversight pages do not show content, and the space's own pages, including **Open space**, still require membership exactly as before. Testing a web widget's answers counts as content too, because the answers draw on the space's knowledge.

To work with a space's content, join it; see [Join a space](#join-a-space). An organisation administrator who is already a member, directly or through a user group, sees what their role allows, like any other member.

Two routes reach content without the join record. Include them in your routine:

- **User groups.** An administrator who adds themselves to a user group under **Admin → User Groups** gets that group's role in every space the group is a member of. The audit log records the group change, but the space's members see no join notice.
- **Tenant-scoped service keys.** A service API key with tenant scope reaches every space's content; see [API access](#api-access).

## Manage members

On the **Members** tab you add people and groups, change their roles and remove them, without joining the space. Each change takes effect at once and is always recorded in the audit log.

- **Add person** and **Add group** add a member with a role. People and groups that are already members are marked **Already a member**.
- Change a role with the role menu on the member's row. **Viewer** reads the knowledge and chats with published assistants. **Editor** can also change assistants and knowledge, and sees insights about users' questions where insights are on. **Administrator** can also manage the space's members and settings.
- Remove a member from the member's row. Removing a person also revokes the API keys that person has for the space. Removing a group revokes no API keys; a person's own API keys only work in spaces where the person is still a member.

Each person is listed with name, email and role, and with badges for **Organisation administrator**, **Joined through oversight** (with the date and the reason), **Inactive account** and **Invited**. Each group shows how many people it has.

Two rules apply to every change:

- **A space keeps at least one administrator.** A change that would leave the space without an administrator who can act is refused: demoting or removing the last one, including the last administrator group, or leaving (see [Leave a space](#leave-a-space)). Only active and invited accounts with the **Administrator** role count, directly or through a group; an inactive or deleted account cannot manage anything. Appoint another administrator first. A space that already has no administrator can always be fixed.
- **You change your own access only by joining or leaving.** You cannot add yourself, change your own role or remove yourself on the **Members** tab. Adding a group you belong to, or giving it a higher role, is refused as well when it would raise your own role; lowering or removing such a group is allowed. Joining records your reason and shows the space's members that you joined, and leaving is recorded as well.

If another administrator changed the member list while you had the page open, Eneo tells you and shows the current list. Member changes require you to be signed in to Eneo; API keys cannot make them.

## Join a space

Join a space when you need its content, for example to investigate a report, check a document or help the space's editors. Choose **Join space…** at the top of the space's oversight page, or in the preview section of a web widget's review page.

1. Choose a role. If you are already a member through a group, only roles above your group role are offered. Choose the lowest role that is enough for the task; the lowest available role is preselected.
2. Write a reason, 10–500 characters. Refer to a case number instead of writing personal data: the reason is shown to the space's administrators and to other organisation administrators, and it is stored in the audit log. Line breaks are saved as spaces.
3. Choose **Join as** followed by the role, for example **Join as Viewer**.

After joining, the space is in your space selector and you work in it like any other member with the same role. Every member of the space sees that you joined, when and with which role: the space's overview page shows the notice **Organisation administrator in this space**, and the members page marks you **Joined through oversight** with the date. On those pages only the space's administrators see your reason. The join is always recorded in the audit log, with the role and the reason.

If you are already a member through a group, joining adds a direct membership with a higher role, and the higher role applies. When a group already gives you **Administrator**, there is nothing to join. If you already have a direct membership, you cannot join again or change your own role: another organisation administrator can change it, or you can leave and join again with a new reason.

The join record belongs to the membership. If you later lose the **Admin** permission you stay an ordinary member, and the **Members** tab still shows **Joined through oversight** but no longer **Organisation administrator**, which makes such memberships easy to find; another organisation administrator can remove them. If the space's own administrators remove you and add you again, the new membership has no join record.

## Leave a space

Choose **Leave space…** on the space's oversight page when you are done. You lose access to the space's documents and assistants, the API keys you have for the space are revoked, and the space stays visible under **Admin → Spaces**. If you are also a member through a group, you keep that group's role; the dialog tells you so. Leaving is always recorded in the audit log, including whether you had joined through oversight.

You can leave any direct membership this way, not only one you got by joining. You cannot leave while you are the space's only administrator: appoint another administrator first.

## Spaces that need attention

The top of **Admin → Spaces** shows what needs attention:

- **Spaces without an administrator.** Nobody in such a space can add members or change its settings. **Show these spaces** filters the list to them. Fix it on the space's **Members** tab: give a member the **Administrator** role or add a person as administrator. You do not need to join. These spaces are also marked in the list and on their own page.
- **Web widgets waiting for activation.** Up to three requests are listed, each with a **Review** link; **Show all in Web widgets** opens the full list.

When there is nothing to do, the section says **Nothing needs attention right now.**

A space counts as having no administrator when no active or invited account has the **Administrator** role in it, directly or through a group.

## Review widget requests

Editors cannot activate a web widget themselves. They request activation in the widget editor, and an organisation administrator reviews the widget and then activates it or sends the request back with a message. You do not need to be a member of the space to review a widget or decide on it. Testing the widget's answers does require membership, because the answers draw on the space's knowledge. See [Review activation requests](/guides/embed-widget#review-activation-requests) in the web widget guide.

## What the audit log records

These actions are recorded in the audit log:

| Action in the audit log                      | Recorded when                                                             | Always logged |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Joined a space through oversight             | An organisation administrator joins a space, with role and reason         | Yes           |
| Left a space (oversight)                     | An organisation administrator leaves a space                              | Yes           |
| Member added by organisation administrator   | A person or group is added through oversight                              | Yes           |
| Role changed by organisation administrator   | A person's or group's role is changed through oversight                   | Yes           |
| Member removed by organisation administrator | A person or group is removed through oversight                            | Yes           |
| Widget activation request sent back          | An administrator sends an activation request back, with the message       | Yes           |
| Widget activation requested                  | An editor requests activation of a web widget                             | No            |
| Widget activation request withdrawn          | An editor withdraws a request                                             | No            |

Activating, pausing and archiving a web widget are always logged too.

**Always logged** means that the action is recorded even when audit logging is turned off for the organisation, whatever the category and action settings. Its switch in the audit configuration is locked and marked **Always logged**. The entry is written together with the change: if it cannot be saved, the change is not made. The organisation's audit log retention applies to these entries like any other.

An organisation may reduce the logging of ordinary activity, but not of access and changes made without membership, or of publishing to the open web. The administrators who could make such changes can also change the audit settings, so a record they could switch off would not be a control.

Each entry names the administrator, the space and, where they apply, the member or group and the roles. A join reason or a send-back message is kept in the entry's details (its metadata), never in its description. Opening the oversight pages is not logged: reading settings, members, instructions or usage leaves no entry.

Review joins regularly, for example once a month. Filter the audit log on the action **Joined a space through oversight**, and check that each membership is still needed. **Where I'm a member** on **Admin → Spaces** shows your own memberships. Check changes to user groups as well (see [Content requires membership](#content-requires-membership)).

## API access

Reading the list of spaces, a space's details and a web widget's review page also works with an API key that has tenant scope and `admin` permission. Every change requires a signed-in session: member changes, joining and leaving, and requesting, withdrawing and sending back widget activation. API keys get `403` with `code: "session_auth_required"`, so a key can never make itself a member of a space.

Service keys are an exception to the membership rule. A service key with tenant scope gets its role in **every** space in the organisation (`read` gives Viewer, `write` gives Editor, `admin` gives Administrator), content included, without joining and without the space's members seeing it. "Content only after a recorded join" therefore applies to people, not to such keys. Create tenant-scoped service keys only for vetted integrations, and prefer a key scoped to a single space. See [How Service Keys Derive Space Roles](/docs/api-key-management#how-service-keys-derive-space-roles).

## Privacy

Oversight shows what administrators need for governance and keeps people's work out of view:

- **Small numbers are hidden.** Question and user figures for the past 30 days are shown only when at least 5 different signed-in people asked questions or ran apps in the space in that period. Below that, the summary says **Not shown – fewer than 5 active users**, so that no individual's usage can be read from the figures. Questions from anonymous web widget visitors are counted separately and always shown.
- **Activity is a period, not a time.** The last activity is shown as past week, past month, past 3 months, more than 3 months ago or no activity.
- **Instructions can contain personal data.** Every organisation administrator, and every API key with tenant scope and `admin` permission, can read assistant and app instructions, and reading them is not logged. Write instructions without personal data, and cover this access in your data protection impact assessment.
- **Document titles stay with members.** Knowledge is shown as sources, counts and sizes only, because even a title can contain personal data.
- **Reasons are read by others.** A join reason is shown to the space's administrators and to other organisation administrators, and it is stored in the audit log. Refer to a case number instead of naming people.

These safeguards limit what the figures reveal; they are not anonymisation. In a very small space, repeated observation can still narrow things down.
